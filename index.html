1st Workshop on Structured Physical Intelligence (SPIN)

Does physical intelligence need structure?

CoRL 2026  |  Austin, Texas, USA  |  November 12, 2026
JW Room 208 (Level 2)

Overview

What does it take to achieve the physical intelligence of a five-year-old? Children learn to manipulate new objects and generalize skills from only a handful of interactions. Despite rapid progress in vision and language, today’s robots remain far less data-efficient and lack comparably rich training data.

Structured Physical Intelligence (SPIN) asks how physical priors and structured world representations can close this gap. Relevant approaches include Visual-Language-Action models (VLAs), world models, object-centric and compositional representations, affordances, and particle-based models. Robotics provides a direct test of whether these ideas support efficient generalization for dexterous manipulation, hierarchical control, and few-shot learning.

The workshop brings researchers together to examine recent advances, current limitations, and open problems. The workshop will feature a live dexterous manipulation challenge that puts today’s robots side by side with a child, with invited talks and a panel discussion.

Research Questions

  • Q1 — What structure enables data-efficient physical intelligence?
  • Q2 — How should physical agents represent the world?
  • Q3 — What should be built in, and what should emerge from scale?
  • Q4 — How can structured models generalize and adapt?
  • Q5 — How should progress be understood and evaluated?

The Child–Robot Dexterity Challenge

How do today’s robots compare with a child on everyday manipulation tasks?

We invite researchers, students, and practitioners from academia and industry to test their robotic systems alongside a child on the same everyday household tasks. We will compare performance task by task to show what today’s robots can and cannot do and help participants learn from one another.

How it works

We will select three to five everyday manipulation tasks that a child can complete with familiar objects. We will share the final task list and simple completion guidelines before the event so teams can prepare.

Key dates and participation details

  • Final tasks and rules: To be announced. Example tasks may include grasping and moving everyday objects, untwisting a bottle cap, inserting an object into a matching opening, folding a piece of cloth, and pouring from one container into another.
  • Challenge launch: September 17, 2026
  • Registration deadline: October 12, 2026
  • Live challenge: November 12, 2026 — Griffin Ballroom

Prize pool

The challenge features a $1,000 prize pool, sponsored by Scale AI.

Hardware

We will provide a bimanual system with two YAM arms, based on the MolmoAct 2 setup, together with a dexterous Sharpa hand setup. Sharpa will support up to five teams with remote experiments and data collection.

Evaluation

We will share completion guidelines and details on timing, attempts, resets, and autonomy with teams before the event. We will provide a short, task-by-task summary of the demonstrations, with a focus on friendly comparison and shared learning.

The child and participating robotic systems will try the same tasks, giving the audience a familiar point of comparison.

Structured World Models as Scalable Data Engines for Robotics

Yunzhu Li

Structured World Models as Scalable Data Engines for Robotics

Abstract: Scaling robotic manipulation demands predictive models of how the world evolves under action and scalable sources of diverse training data. In this talk, I will present structured world models that incorporate physical priors for planning and control across rigid, deformable, articulated, and granular objects. I will also introduce a real-to-sim-to-real framework that turns real-world observations into interactive digital twins for scalable data generation, policy training, and evaluation, laying the groundwork for more general and robust robotic foundation models.
